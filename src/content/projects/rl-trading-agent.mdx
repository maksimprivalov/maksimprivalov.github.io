## Problem

A research project, not a production trading system. I wanted to understand three things hands-on:
how to frame a financial problem as a Markov decision process, why reinforcement learning for trading
is hard (sparse signal, non-stationary markets, easy-to-miss data leakage), and how to build an ML
pipeline end to end with honest benchmarking.

The agent observes a rolling window of market features and decides each day whether to **BUY**,
**HOLD** or **SELL**. Default asset: MSFT, daily data from 2015 to 2025.

### Environment

- **Observation:** a `window_size × 6` vector of the last N days of `log_return`, `sma20`, `sma50`,
  `rsi14`, `macd` and `volume_change`.
- **Actions:** discrete: `SELL`, `HOLD`, `BUY`.
- **Position sizing:** all-in / all-out, on purpose, to keep the action space small enough to learn.
- **Costs:** a fixed 0.1% fee on buys and sells. Slippage and market impact are not modeled.

| Split      | Period            | Purpose                      |
| ---------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Train      | 2015-01 → 2020-12 | Policy learning              |
| Test       | 2021-01 → 2023-12 | Out-of-sample evaluation     |
| Validation | 2024-01 → 2024-12 | Reward tuning, sanity checks |

## Key decisions

- **Shaped reward instead of raw PnL.** Pure PnL produced an agent that learned to do nothing, because
  doing nothing never pays a fee. The reward combines equity change, a bonus for closing a winning
  trade, a reward for holding during upward moves, penalties for invalid actions and for sitting flat,
  and a small volatility penalty. It's pragmatic, not principled.
- **Strictly chronological splits.** No shuffling. A first version computed indicators per split,
  which made the 20-day SMA at the start of the test set wrong; computing indicators on the full
  series before splitting fixed that leak.
- **Buy & Hold as the benchmark.** Beating a strongly trending stock with a long-only discrete policy
  is hard by design. The agent is competitive with Buy & Hold in some training runs and underperforms
  in others; results are sensitive to reward coefficients and the random seed.

## What I learned

Reward design dominated everything else: more time went into reward coefficients than PPO
hyperparameters. 100k timesteps was too few for stable policies. The honest next steps are
continuous actions for position sizing, walk-forward evaluation across market regimes, multi-seed
runs, and Sharpe ratio and max drawdown as metrics instead of raw equity.
